Version 6 (13 September 2026). Changes from version 5, in the order a reader should care about them.

**The paper now leads with the framework and reports the aircraft as the case that instantiates it.** Version 5 opened its abstract and highlights with the configuration and a mass advantage of thirty-seven to forty-two percent. That ordering did not match the paper's own contributions list, which had said since version 4 that the primary contribution is a framework and the aircraft the case; and it did not match the evidence, because the mass figure rests on a drag assumption this version brackets by calculation. The abstract and highlights now lead with the three currencies, the transfer property and the escape condition, and state the framework's central prediction — that architectural rankings belong to sizing contracts rather than to architectures. The case study is unchanged in extent and reported in full, including the two occasions on which its own audit found against it. No claim of general architectural superiority is made anywhere in the paper, and that is now stated explicitly.

**Four calculations were run that earlier versions had named as missing, and three of them changed a claim.**

*The drag of the attitude rotors in cruise was computed rather than assumed.* A blade is designed for its hover duty by blade-element momentum theory and the same blade is then run at cruise speed in the zero-shaft-torque state a free-wheeling fixed-pitch propeller settles into. Version 5 reported 0.0085 for the eight discs. That figure came from the most favourable of four designs, whose hover figure of merit is 0.27 against the 0.599 on which every mass and energy result in the paper is built — a propeller the aircraft's own power budget excludes. The figure of merit is flat between 0.63 and 0.65 up to a design section lift coefficient of 0.68 and then collapses, so 0.599 falls inside the collapse and no blade in the family sits on it; every design that meets the hover requirement gives at least **0.0154**. A compressibility correction was applied to the section polar and the zero-torque shaft speed searched again, moving the figure by four tenths of one percent: a free-wheeling blade sits at almost zero section lift, which carries its drag-divergence Mach number above the speed it turns at. The figure is therefore reported as a value and not as a bound.

*The zero-lift drag build-up was corrected twice and the assumption no longer survives it.* The build-up contained no rotor row at all. Adding one, and using in both columns the only rotor the aircraft can actually carry, gives a bracket of **0.0285 to 0.0381**. The assumed 0.0248 lies below both ends. Three sections of version 5 called that assumption conservative; it is optimistic at the favourable end of the paper's own calculation, and the paper now says so.

*The comparative sizing was re-derived across that bracket.* Version 5 charged this configuration 1/1.12 for exposed hardware, covering the tip frames alone, while charging the lift-plus-cruise layout a ratio taken from a measured configuration that already contains its rotor drag. The comparison was asymmetric and the asymmetry had never been quantified. Rebuilt from one drag book-keeping — which reproduces the published clean-body lift-to-drag ratio to within a third of a percent before anything is changed — the cruise ratio falls from 11.88 to between 10.82 and 8.80 and the range of the light reference design from a single 1 583 km to **1 173 to 1 442 km**. Under equal fuel fractions the lift-plus-cruise layout now flies further across the whole bracket; under fixed take-off mass this configuration leads across the whole bracket; under fixed fuel mass the sign changes inside it. **The contract-dependence result therefore survives the bracket and is strengthened by it**, which is the finding the paper leads on. A second correction runs the other way: applying a measured battery pack's buffer fraction to all three architectures rather than to this one alone, the lift-plus-cruise layout stops closing between eighteen and twenty percent of take-off mass, so what the measured pack costs is not this configuration's margin but the competing configuration's existence.

*A Reynolds-averaged solution of the planform was run and compared with the vortex-lattice loading.* This is the calculation five earlier versions listed as the one exposure in the aerodynamic chain with no bound. The local loading ratio holds to within five percent of a constant across nine tenths of the span, which is the first direct evidence that the method's magnitude error is largely multiplicative — the condition the paper's cancellation argument requires and had never been able to test. The overall ratio runs opposite to the published comparison the paper cites, and the paper reports that rather than reconciling it. Converted into the units of the loading-shape sensitivity, the residual is 1.6 degrees of equivalent redistribution against a 2.6-degree threshold, or 5.3 degrees if one station near the tip is retained; a single station carries the difference, and the paper reports both figures rather than choosing the one that suits it.

**Smaller corrections, each of which a reader can check.** The neutral point was quoted from the untwisted planform while the sensitivity study used the wing twisted to trim; chasing that discrepancy found a scatter of 1.3 percent of mean chord across equally defensible solver choices, five times the grid sensitivity and never previously measured, so static margins are now quoted to one decimal and the neutral-point column of the sensitivity table is marked as sitting inside its own method's noise floor. The ledger of what the configuration pays said the attitude propellers do not sit in the cruise airstream; they do, and they are now its largest entry. A dead roadmap sentence left over from the IMRaD restructure claimed that Section 1 reviews seventy years of attempts, which is self-referential and false. The transition section's rotational-dynamics result, which removes the zero-altitude-loss finding of the point-mass model, was missing from the conclusions.

**What has not changed.** The framework, the escape condition, the four-part statement of it, and the finding that the ranking of architectures reverses between sizing contracts. The aircraft is still not shown to be flyable, and the paper says so in the abstract, in the highlights and in the conclusions.
