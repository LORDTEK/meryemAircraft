Version 5 (12 September 2026). Changes from version 4, in the order a reader should care about them.

**The paper is restructured to IMRaD** — Introduction, Materials and Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusions — which is the section list the target journal requires. This is a move, not a rewrite: all forty-one subsections survive, no paragraph was deleted, and the roughly one hundred and eighty cross-references were remapped in a single pass and then machine-checked against the surviving headings. Obligatory front and back matter absent from version 4 has been written: Highlights, Author Contributions in CRediT form, a Supplementary Materials statement, a Patents statement, and a dual-use note. The abstract is cut from 588 words to 220.

**Four corrections change a number that mattered.** First, the comparative sizing table in the body was not the paper's own sizing at all: it was an external comparison of three flying eVTOLs reproduced from the literature, with the column headers altered. The real three-architecture, three-contract result now stands in its place, and the external comparison is returned to the supplement under its own labels. Second, hover power was stated at thrust equal to weight — that is, at a thrust-to-weight ratio of 1.00 — while two other places assumed 1.20; the installed power supplies no climb at all, and the margin to leave the ground comes from the four attitude-control propellers, which raises the achievable ratio to between 1.066 and 1.132 depending on how much rotation authority is held in reserve. The transition results were recomputed at the achievable ratios: the altitude-loss result survives at both reference rotation times and at every ratio down to 1.00, but acquiring the entry climb now costs 3.9 s and 9.6 m rather than 2.6 s and 6.4 m, a rotation begun from rest is markedly worse than reported, and the take-off margin and the attitude authority are drawn from the same four propellers and compete. Third, the battery buffer's specific power was obtained by subtracting the engine's shaft rating from the rotor's shaft power and dividing by the buffer mass — three quantities at three different stations. Taken at the electrical bus, where the buffer is, the requirement is 5.63 kW/kg to hover and 6.48 to leave the ground, against a highest measured rate of about 1.5 kW/kg on a flown pack: a factor of 3.8 rather than the 3.1 implied before. Fourth, the tip propellers are no longer described as producing attitude moments and nothing else, because they no longer do.

**One source request closed, and it closed against the configuration.** The tip-frame fairing that supplies the aircraft's only directional stability is a symmetric section of 39 mm chord at a chord Reynolds number near 80,000, and its side force was computed from an assumed lift-curve slope of 4 per radian. A measurement compilation already in the reference list reports that symmetric sections at that Reynolds number carry a deadband in the lift curve near zero incidence, absent at higher Reynolds numbers and absent on cambered sections — and a toe angle of one to two degrees places the fairing inside that band. The assumed slope is therefore an upper bound rather than a conservative choice, because what the measurements remove is the linearity of the curve and not merely the magnitude of its slope. The same source names the remedy, which costs nothing structurally: camber the fairing outboard and keep the toe-out. The fairing is not resized here, because choosing a slope from a curve that has not been measured is the error the correction exists to record.

**Smaller corrections, each of which a reader can check.** Two citations were misattributed, and the numbers behind one of them were verified correct against the source while the surrounding claim was not: the cited sizing table does not show a dedicated lift system paying in cruise efficiency, it shows the weight charge surviving a seventy percent better cruise efficiency, which is the sharper prediction and is what the text now makes. The zero-lift drag build-up table was missing its excrescence row and did not sum to its own total. The conservative rotation margin is 1.14 rather than 1.59, and 0.76 on a smooth profile. Control power scales as the inverse cube of rotation time, not the inverse square. Range does contain propulsive efficiency, and correcting that moves the comparison against the tilting layout rather than for it. Two places asserted that no remaining analysis required an experiment while the conclusion said the opposite.

**What has not changed.** The framework, the escape condition, the contract-dependence result, and the finding that the ranking of architectures reverses between sizing contracts. The aircraft is still not shown to be flyable, and the paper says so in the abstract, in the highlights, and in the conclusion.

---

Hybrid vertical take-off and landing (VTOL) aircraft pay for runway independence in cruise efficiency. This paper treats that cost as architectural rather than as a defect of implementation and develops it as an accounting framework: the penalty is charged in three coupled currencies — hover hardware carried through cruise, its drag when exposed, and continuous power sized by a condition holding for some two percent of the flight — and each remedy surveyed reduces one by raising another. The escape condition follows in four parts: the same hardware, in the same orientation, doing the same job, with the hover peak drawn from a buffer. meryemAircraft, an uncrewed tail-sitting blended-wing body with one coaxial nose pair and four tip pairs, instantiates it and is sized at 50 kg and 1000 kg from one set of equations. Against a lift-plus-cruise layout, on wind-tunnel drag, it closes the same mission at forty-two percent lower take-off mass; against a tilting layout the ranking reverses between sizing contracts, so three are reported rather than one. The aircraft is not shown to be flyable: its mass budget closes only on a battery specific power 3.8 times the highest rate yet measured on a flown pack, and transition controllability rests on a pitching moment no current method predicts reliably. The framework does not depend on that outcome.
