Version 7 (14 September 2026). Changes from version 6, in the order a reader should care about them. **Version 6 is not withdrawn and its DOI continues to resolve; this version corrects it.**

**The correction that motivated this version is one sentence, and it was an error in version 6's favour of caution rather than in its own favour.** Version 6's introduction stated that "no claim of general architectural superiority is made anywhere in this paper." That sentence was inserted as scope discipline. It was wrong on the paper's own terms: it denied two claims the paper earns and it contradicted Section 4.2 of the same document, which argues a capability band against the two families that bound it. It was added without the corresponding author's agreement and it removed the statement of why the configuration exists. It has been replaced by a structure that separates what is claimed from what is not.

**The paper now makes three architectural claims, each against a different family on a different axis, and declines a fourth.** Against runway-dependent fixed-wing aircraft: it needs no prepared surface. Against multirotors: it cruises on a wing, which is a structural difference rather than a margin. Against tilting architectures: it reaches the same regime transition **without reorienting a propulsor** — no pivot, no nacelle actuator, no variable-pitch hub, no retraction mechanism, no gyroscopic moment from tilting mass; the airframe rotates and the propulsors hold one orientation from take-off to cruise. What is *not* claimed is a range ranking against the other hybrid VTOL architectures, because Section 3.6 shows that ranking reverses with the sizing contract and reports the tilting layout leading under all three. The criterion that separates the three from the fourth is stated in the paper: each claim names something the competitor structurally lacks, and none of those is a quantity a sizing contract can move.

**The third claim is deliberately narrower than it first appeared, and the narrowing is itself a correction made during this version.** A first attempt at it said "no mechanism that moves," "no aerodynamic control surface," and "every moment about every axis is produced by differential thrust." All three are false of this aircraft and are contradicted by its own Section 2.10: roll cannot be produced by coaxial torque-balanced propellers at any setting, and is produced instead by a variable-extension strip on the lower surface — the one moving aerodynamic device the aircraft carries. Pitch and yaw are differential thrust; roll is not. The claim as it now stands is the elimination of a **mechanism class**, the one that reorients a propulsor between hover and cruise, with the strip named in the same sentence in every place the claim appears. The paper does not claim mechanical simplicity, lower mass, higher reliability or lower maintenance: none of those was measured, and the paper says so.

**Numerical content is unchanged from version 6.** The drag bracket 0.0285–0.0381, the cruise lift-to-drag range 8.8–10.8, the 32 to 36 percent mass advantage, the thrust-to-weight ratios 1.066 / 1.041 / 1.132 / 1.082, the heavy ranges 1 571 km charged and 1 814 km published, the converged loading ratio 0.796 and the light rotor charge 0.0154 all stand exactly as deposited. One heavy-line figure is now reported differently rather than differently computed: version 6 carried the free-wheeling charge of the heavy tip rotors as a single 0.0051, and re-running the selection rule showed it does not bind at that scale — the figure of merit collapses above a design section lift coefficient of 0.68 on the light line and pins the choice, but does not collapse on the heavy line, so the rule returns whichever design sits at the end of the range swept. The charge is now reported as **bounded by 0.0035 and 0.0074**, with 0.0051 identified as the interior value the sizing was run at rather than as a minimum. Applying the rule literally would have returned the lowest of these, which is the end favourable to this configuration; that is why it was not done silently.

**Several internal inconsistencies were found and corrected, and they are listed because a reader checking version 6 will find them there.** Section 3.5 called the tip frames the largest single payment the configuration makes, when Section 3.3 had already computed the free-wheeling rotors at 0.0154 against the frames' 0.0043; the frames are now the second largest. Section 4.5 described the computed drag bracket as surrounding the assumed 0.0248, which was true of a superseded bracket and is not true of this one, which sits entirely above it. Two pointers sent a reader to Section 3.2 for a sizing set that is in Section 3.1. Two more sent a reader to Section 3.11 for component figures that are in the supplementary material. Section 3.3's prose said four propeller designs where its table lists seven. Table 1's caption said four wind-tunnel configurations where the table lists three. Table 18 presented a coarse-mesh loading ratio without saying so. The conclusions quoted a thrust-to-weight range covering only the light design. Sections 3.15 and its figure disagreed about which thrust-to-weight ratios the installed power supplies, and the figure has been redrawn at the ratios the aircraft actually has.

**Two omissions are now stated rather than left to be noticed.** No drag bracket has been computed for the heavy line, which stands on its own zero-lift drag coefficient of 0.0200; the comparison of the heavy range against the light bracket is therefore asymmetric and is stated in the weakest form the evidence supports. And the torque balance of the coaxial pairs is exact at cruise rather than hover, leaving a residual in hover whose trim this study does not establish.

**Three open items were added to the limitations, none of which existed in version 6's list.** The landing transition is unmodelled and is not symmetric with the take-off transition. The choice of free-wheeling over stopping the tip rotors edge-on is unpriced, although the drag difference is a factor of twenty. And the roll strip's actuation is unsized — no mass, no power, no bandwidth, no hinge — while the inboard forty-six percent of the strip sits inside the nose propeller's slipstream, which is both where its hover authority comes from and a high-frequency pulsating load environment.

**Production.** Figures were renumbered to first-mention order, which version 6 did not follow; twenty-two tables were numbered, captioned and cited, of which version 6 numbered two; four blocks of unrendered LaTeX were converted; the mandatory highlights block, present in version 6's PDF, was absent from its Word rendering and is now in both.

**What has not changed.** The framework, the three currencies, the transfer property, the escape condition and the finding that the ranking of architectures reverses between sizing contracts. The aircraft is still not shown to be flyable: the reference budget needs about 3.8 times the highest battery specific power yet measured on a flown pack, and the transition rests on a pitching moment that three methods at three fidelities fail to predict. The paper says so in the abstract, the highlights and the conclusions.
