meryemAircraft is a tail-sitting blended-wing body that takes off and lands vertically and cruises on its wing. It changes regime by rotating the airframe rather than its propulsors. Every propulsor is a coaxial, torque-balanced pair of fixed-pitch propellers, and none changes its orientation relative to the airframe, or its blade pitch, at any point in the flight: the aircraft carries no pivot or tilting joint, no nacelle actuator, no variable-pitch hub and no dedicated lift rotors. Pitch and yaw come from differential thrust between the tip pairs; roll comes from a strip on the lower surface. A buffered series hybrid supplies the hover peak.

The paper presents the architecture, sizes a 50 kg reference design, and audits it with an accounting of carried hover mass, exposed cruise drag and hover-sized continuous power; one prediction of that accounting is checked against an independent NASA sizing study of nine VTOL designs. The effective lift-to-drag ratio, 5.56 to 7.39 before sizing closure, exceeds a published turboshaft quadrotor's throughout and ranges from 4 percent below to 27 percent above an all-electric one. The sizing loop closes at 52.3 to 57.5 kilograms.

The configuration is aimed at wildfire observation and response, and at cargo delivery to places without a runway.

Files: the paper; the supplement, which carries the working behind the paper's pointers (S1 to S11); and the LaTeX source of both.
