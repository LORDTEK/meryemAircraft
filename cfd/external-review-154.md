## 0. The author read W-2, and did not like what we had made of it

I put (ii), (iii) and (iv) to the author side by side. The author's answer, in my translation from Turkish (so not a verbatim quotation):

> *What is this? It reads like a legal text. I could not follow who argues what. A person reading a sentence this complicated will not

**It does not.** All three candidates were long for one reason: all five of us tried to keep a three-item list after *"the architecture"*.
The author then asked me why Sections 1 and 5.1 should conflict at all, and proposed one sentence that joins them (again my translation):

> *The contribution this paper offers is the architecture that brings the advantages together, the condition and the price.*

**Half of that is exactly right.** *"The architecture that brings the advantages together"* dissolves the conflict I had set up. The
combination is not a rival to the architecture; it is **what the architecture does**. Section 6.2 already says so: *"a configuration sized
to combine runway-independent vertical operation with wing-borne cruise efficiency, arranged to do so with no mechanism that reorients a
propulsor"*.

**The other half has a cost, and I told the author so.** *"… the architecture, the condition and the price"* again counts three
contributions. The paper's standing decision (Round 35) is one: the framework, and with it the condition, is the instrument, not a second
contribution. Sections 1, 6.2 and 9 all say one. The author accepted that, and accepted the middle way I proposed. **The author's sentence
is kept, but the contribution stays single.** The condition and the price are the measures of the contribution, not parts of it.

### 0.1 The candidate — the author's, in the form the author approved

> **(vi)** *"What this paper contributes is an architecture that brings the three elements together; the condition shows what it
> satisfies, and the price what it costs."*

In place of the current protected sentence in 5.1:

> *"**What this paper contributes is that combination, the condition its primary propulsor is designed to satisfy, and the price the
> configuration pays for pursuing it.**"*

**The author's reason, which the author asked me to put to you in so many words** (my translation): *This is not a legal text. Once it
cannot be understood, being right loses its importance.*

**Why it reads better, and what it keeps:**
- **One contribution.** It agrees with Sections 1, 6.2 and 9.
- **ChatGPT's concern disappears.** The condition is no longer something *"presented and priced"*. It is the standard, the thing that
  *"shows what it satisfies"*, which is what ChatGPT said the condition is.
- **No new predicate that I can find:**
  - *"the three elements"* is the subject of the sentence immediately before (*"None of the three elements is new"*) and the sentence
    immediately after (*"The three elements, taken together, meet the escape condition …"*);
  - that the architecture brings them together is Section 5's own title, *Combining the solutions*, and 6.2's *"sized to combine"*;
  - what the condition shows is stated in the next sentence;
  - what the price is, Section 7.2 prices.
- **It is short.** A reviewer reads it once.

**Two things I want you to check, because I may be too close to it:**
1. **"an architecture" or "the architecture"?** Section 1 defines *"the architecture"*. The indefinite article is natural before a relative
   clause (*"an architecture that brings …"*), but could a reader take it for a second, different architecture? My view: no. The relative
   clause identifies it, and the definite form *"the architecture that brings the three elements together"* is equally correct. Choose.
2. **"and the price what it costs"** is an elliptical clause (the verb *shows* is understood). It is standard English. If you think a
   non-native reviewer stumbles on it, the full form is *"and the price shows what it costs"*.

**Please answer:** (a) is there a new predicate or a lost one in (vi)? (b) the article; (c) the ellipsis. **The author has approved the
approach.** Your job is the check, and answering each other on it.

**For the record:** (ii), (iii) and (iv) are withdrawn in favour of (vi), unless your check finds a defect in it. The Q1 decision (no comma)
is moot, because (vi) has no such comma.
