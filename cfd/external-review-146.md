## 3. Three proposed body changes (R), each to vote

### W — Step 1's route item: the literature already names the mechanical reason

**The item now:**
> **The route itself is established.** Uncrewed tail-sitters combining fixed-pitch rotors with a flying wing have been built and flown
> for more than a decade, beginning with quadrotor-plus-wing arrangements carrying a few aerodynamic actuators for forward flight.

**Proposed addition** (P122 form, with the scope on the same line):
> *A tail-sitter study reported in 2007 already gives the reason the route is taken: tilting configurations reach the same goal "at the
> expense of significantly increased mechanical complexity compared to a tail-sitter that uses propeller wash over normal aircraft control
> surfaces to effect vertical flight control."*

**Why:**
- Four sources we have now read say that tail-sitting avoids the tilting mechanism: Escareno 2007 p. 3385, Escareno 2008 p. 262, Oosedo 2013
  p. 317, and Vegh manuscript p. 4. None of them is in the body saying so.
- Step 1 says *"that distinction is what this paper is built on"*. Without W, a reader could take the mechanical distinction for our own
  insight. Our rule is to state what is occupied *before* the gap.
- **The quotation runs to its end on purpose.** It names a tail-sitter *"that uses propeller wash over normal aircraft control surfaces"*, and
  this paper refuses exactly that. Cutting it at *"a tail-sitter"* would be the selective-quotation class (S-54).

**My vote: yes.** It narrows what we claim and widens nothing.

**Please check one thing:** does *"already gives the reason the route is taken"* go further than the source, which states a comparison,
not a reason for anyone's choice? The alternative is *"already states the comparison:"*.

### E-1 — Step 1's coaxial item: its cost clause, in the primary's own words

**The item's first sentence now:**
> **Coaxial contra-rotating propulsion on a tail-sitter is established**, proposed specifically to remove the reaction torque a single
> propeller imposes, at the cost of an extra motor and the coaxial arrangement.

**Where it stands:**
- The reason clause is verified primary (2008 p. 262).
- *"at the cost of an extra motor and the coaxial arrangement"* is De Wagter's wording (secondary).
- The primary names the cost in other words: *"it increases the mechanical complexity"*.
- The source-opening rule says a primary qualification found this way is either quoted in the body or recorded as omitted, with a reason.

**Options:**
- **(a)** Keep the wording, and record the primary's *"mechanical complexity"* as omitted. The reason would be that it is the same cost in
  different words.
- **(b)** Use the primary's words:
  > **Coaxial contra-rotating propulsion on a tail-sitter is established**, proposed specifically to face the reaction torque a single
  > propeller imposes *"without using complementary controls"*, at a cost its proposers name directly: it *"increases the mechanical
  > complexity."*

**My vote: (b).**
- Our contribution sits on the mechanism axis. A primary source that says a coaxial pair adds mechanical complexity belongs in front of
  the reader, not in a record.
- The paper already answers part of it: Step 8 says each rotor on its own machine makes *"the splitting gearbox and the mechanical governors"*
  unnecessary.
- The paper claims a count of mechanism classes, not simplicity (Section 9 item 4). (b) does not contradict that.
- The next sentence (*"… states the same purpose in the same terms"*) still holds, because the purpose is still torque compensation.

### N9 — Step 1, last paragraph: "three decades"

**Now:**
> *"blended wing bodies have been a standing subject of transport research for three decades"*

The source dates the start to 1988, which is 38 years ago.

**Option:** *"… for more than three decades"*.

**My vote: yes.** It is exact, and it adds no new claim.
