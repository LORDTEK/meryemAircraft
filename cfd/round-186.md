## 2. My reasons, where I differ or add. Please criticise them

**4, R-b.** Two things are true at once.
- **The three R-a readers are right** that the word lock (Round 99) wants the family term. *"Rotorcraft and multirotors"* lists a family and its own subset as if they were two.
- **ChatGPT is right** that the paper later separates the two populations. Section 8's axis table labels the opponent *"Rotorcraft: multirotors and helicopters"* and gives them different results (*"Claimed against multirotors, and bounded; against helicopters … mixed and no advantage is claimed"*).
- R-b uses the family term and names both populations in Section 8's own words. It costs two words. It is also the first place the helicopter enters as a member of the family the range claim is made against.
- ChatGPT: does R-b meet your concern? Grok, DeepSeek, Qwen: is R-b acceptable, or is R-a better and why?

**10, R, with Grok.** *"Whether one arrangement pays less than it appears to"* is the reader's first promise, and it is ambiguous. It can be read as a promise to rank this arrangement against the others. Section 6.4 declines that ranking without a contract, and Section 8 claims no range against the other hybrids. The price question stays in the sentence.

**22, R, with DeepSeek.** The split changes no word. The 49-word sentence has two predicates: what changed, and who has been exploiting it.

**23, K.** Section 1.4 is a parallel series. Each paragraph opens with its claim in bold and then gives the evidence:
- *"The route itself is established."*
- *"Attitude without aerodynamic control surfaces is established …"*
- *"Coaxial contra-rotating propulsion on a tail-sitter is established …"*
- … and so on.

Cutting 23 leaves the first item as the only one without its claim sentence. Grok, DeepSeek and Qwen gave the same reading (a topic sentence). ChatGPT: does the parallel structure change your mark?

**45, K, against DeepSeek's split.**
- In the present sentence, the audit is **part of what is not established**. The gap is *"the combination taken together with its price"* (44, protected), and *"audited explicitly against …"* is the price half of it.
- The split makes it a new sentence, *"It is audited explicitly …"*. That reads as a present-tense statement about the configuration, or about this paper, and no longer as part of what the literature lacks. The scope of the gap changes.
- The Round 111 rule applies by analogy: a later sentence must inherit a definition's exact scope.
- If the length is the problem, I would rather keep it whole than split it this way. DeepSeek: is there a split that keeps the audit inside *"not established"*?

**47, R (new).**
- *"The axis"* has no referent in 1.5. The nearest axis is in 1.4, sentence 32: *"a moment about the propeller axis."* That is a subsection and fifteen sentences earlier.
- The rewrite borrows 32's own words. It deliberately does not say *roll*: about the body axis, the strip gives roll in cruise and heading in hover (Section 5.2). Only the moment names both regimes correctly.
