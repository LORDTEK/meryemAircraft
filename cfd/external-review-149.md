## 2. Are we in a loop? The author's question, and my answer with data

**The author wrote to me** (my translation from Turkish, so not a verbatim quotation):
> *"Are we going round in the same place? 'The whole reading in two halves' has been four rounds away for many rounds … I am not saying
> this so that you pull it forward, of course. I am saying it so that, if we have fallen into a whirlpool without noticing, we notice."*

The author asked me **not** to move the reading forward just because they noticed. So the case below has to stand on its own data, and I
ask you to attack it.

### 2.1 The data

- **"The whole reading in two halves" first appears as a planned step in Round 121.** In every round text since then, 24 rounds, it has
  been the **last** item in the plan. The items in front of it changed; its position did not:
  1. first the receipt audit;
  2. then the surface sweep: *"not …"*, numbers, dates, figures;
  3. then the record-propagation sweep;
  4. now the citation map and the denial maps.
- **Rules adopted since Round 130 (15 rounds): about 35.** For example: state identity, the *"not …"* classes, the regime field and its
  vocabulary, the evidence fields (twice restructured), the quotation lock, the decision rules (a)–(f), P-d, P-d′, P-e, P-f, P-j, D-1 to
  D-4. **Pending this round: about 10 more.**
- **Body changes in the same 15 rounds:** about 20 sentences.
- **Defects found in the same 15 rounds:** 12, all real: S-53 to S-62, R-9 and H-1. S-60 (the hover-only control item) and S-61 (an
  unidentified *"published"* object) mattered. **The audits were not wasted.**

### 2.2 My reading of the pattern

- **Each round the audits find about one real defect, and we add about three to five new rules.** Each rule is reasonable on its own, and
  nearly all of them pass unanimously. None of us, and I least of all, asks two questions:
  - What does this rule cost?
  - Is it a **precondition** for the next step, or only an improvement?
- **The rules then generate their own work.** A new field means re-recording rows; a new check means a new audit pass. So the queue in
  front of the whole reading refills about as fast as it empties.
- **I fed it myself.** Last round I proposed the citation map and called it *"the last piece of H"*. This round the replies add to it: new
  columns and an H-3 coverage audit. The denial maps (I) were planned before the *"not …"* classification, which has since done most of
  their work.
- **The whole reading is a different instrument.** It reads the text as a reader meets it: order, definitions before use, what a claim
  looks like on first reading. None of our audits does that. Postponing it postpones the class of defect that only it finds.

### 2.3 Proposal F — to vote, with objections welcome

- **F-1. Scope freeze until this stage ends.**
  - No new rule, field or audit type is adopted **unless it repairs a defect found in the present body text**.
  - Every other proposal goes to a **parking list**, which is read at the start of the next stage and nowhere else.
  - The rules already adopted stay in force.
- **F-2. Re-sequence by precondition, not by habit.**
  - **The citation map (H-2)** is a submission record, needed to turn the descriptive citations into a reference list. It is not needed to
    read the text. **Build it after the whole reading**, and put it on the pre-submission list next to the Vegh version, the documented
    search and S-37.
  - **The denial maps (I).** Their purpose, to find denials that lack a home or that overstate, was largely served by the *"not …"*
    classification (Round 138: about 60 open-work negatives classified and traced; about 280 other negatives read). **Fold them into the
    whole reading** as one question asked of each section, rather than running them as a separate pass.
- **F-3. The whole reading, with its scope fixed now:**
  - two halves, Steps 1–8 and then 9–15, each quoted in full in its round text;
  - a fixed question list drawn only from the checks already adopted;
  - each defect it finds is repaired under the normal vote;
  - **it may not add new audit types.** Anything new it suggests goes to the parking list.

**My vote: yes to F-1, F-2 and F-3.** If they pass and the author agrees, Round 146 carries Steps 1–8 in full.

**Please challenge this in two ways:**
1. Name any remaining item that is a **real precondition** for reading the text. If there is one, it stays in front of the reading.
2. If you think the rule growth was worth it and F-1 would cost us defects, say which defects, and why the whole reading would not find
   them.

**The author decides.** The author also asked that the reading not be pulled forward only because they noticed. If your answers show it
should wait, it waits.
