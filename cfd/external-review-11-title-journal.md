# 1. What changed since your last round

You were unanimous: do not reopen CFD, do not run a reduced campaign,
narrow the transition claim. All of it is done.

- **The transition claim is now**: inertial feasibility demonstrated,
  aerodynamic controllability unresolved. Every place that could be read
  as a controllability claim has been reworded.
- **The generic 0.1–0.3 post-stall values** are no longer offered as
  evidence. They are quoted only to establish that the threshold is not
  obviously negligible, and the text says explicitly that no
  configuration-specific value is assumed.
- **The threshold is now an acceptance criterion**: the paper states what
  a future measurement must show, rather than merely noting a gap.
- **One of you extracted something from our own table that we had not
  seen, and it is the best result of that round.** The threshold falls as
  the square of airspeed — 0.17 at 10 m s⁻¹, 0.019 at 30. So the rotation
  must be performed at low dynamic pressure. That is not a limitation
  discovered after the fact: §7.2 already began the transition near hover
  for thrust-margin reasons, and §7.4 already found slower rotation
  better. Both now have a second, independent justification, and the
  paper states it as a design rule.
- **Reduced campaigns are refused in the text, with the reason given**:
  substituting a less-validated model for the dominant missing term.

We also found an internal inconsistency while looking at the title. The
abstract said "no control surfaces"; the body says "no elevons, no
rudder", which is the correct statement, because the deployable roll
strip *is* an aerodynamic control device. The abstract has been corrected
to match the body.

# 2. The title

## Current

> Reducing the Cruise-Efficiency Penalty of Hybrid VTOL: The
> meryemAircraft Tail-Sitting Blended-Wing-Body Configuration with
> Propeller-Only Control

## Why we want to change it

Three reasons, in order of seriousness.

1. **"Propeller-Only Control" is a controllability claim, and
   controllability is the one thing the paper does not demonstrate.** It
   puts the weakest-supported element of the work in the most prominent
   position, and invites a reviewer to attack there first.
2. **It is not literally accurate.** The roll strip is a moving
   aerodynamic device. The body of the paper is careful about this; the
   title is not.
3. **It obscures the actual contribution.** What the paper establishes,
   with measurements, is that a configuration without a dedicated lift
   system avoids two of the three architectural bills, and it supports
   that with a three-dimensional drag solution, a three-architecture
   comparison under three sizing contracts, and a component mass budget.
   None of that is in the title.

The word "propeller" entered the title for a reason worth stating: the
study deliberately excludes jet propulsion, and the author wanted that
visible. That intent can be served without making a control claim.

## Proposed

> **Reducing the Cruise-Efficiency Penalty of Hybrid VTOL:
> meryemAircraft, a Tail-Sitting Blended-Wing-Body Configuration Without
> a Dedicated Lift System**

Why: *"Without a Dedicated Lift System"* is verifiable by inspection
rather than by argument — it is a description, not a claim. It is also
precisely the architectural feature that produces the paper's measured
results. And it makes no assertion about control, so the undemonstrated
item is no longer advertised.

**A constraint you should know about: the name "meryemAircraft" must
remain in the title.** It is not negotiable, so please work within it
rather than proposing titles that drop it.

**Question 2a.** Is the proposed title better, and is there a version you
would prefer that keeps the name? An alternative we considered, which
foregrounds the intellectual contribution rather than the configuration:

> The Architectural Cost of Hybrid VTOL and meryemAircraft, a
> Configuration That Avoids Two of Its Three Bills

# 3. Target journal

We would value your advice here, and we are genuinely open on it. Our
current thinking, offered as a starting point rather than a decision:

**What we think the paper is.** A configuration study with computational
support and no experiment: an architectural argument, a proposed
configuration, a three-dimensional drag calculation with an uncertainty
budget, a comparative sizing against two rival architectures, a component
mass budget, and an explicit set of open items.

**Venues we have been considering**, roughly in order of ambition:

- *Journal of Aircraft* (AIAA) — the traditional home for configuration
  studies, and topically the most natural fit. Our concern is the absence
  of experimental validation.
- *Aerospace Science and Technology* (Elsevier) — strong fit for design
  and configuration work, similar concern.
- *Aerospace* or *Drones* (MDPI) — more receptive to analytical
  configuration studies, faster, open access; we believe both are Q1 in
  at least some indexing, but we have not verified current rankings and
  would welcome correction.
- *Proceedings of the IMechE Part G* or *CEAS Aeronautical Journal* — a
  safer landing, at a lower ranking.

**Question 3a.** Which venue would you aim at, and why? Is the absence of
experimental validation disqualifying at the first two, or is it accepted
for configuration papers there provided the limitations are explicit?

**Question 3b.** If you think the honest answer is that this paper belongs
in a Q2 venue rather than a Q1 one, please say so directly.

# 4. The question that matters most

**Question 4.** Give a realistic assessment of this paper's prospects at a
Q1 journal, as it now stands.

We are asking you to be specific rather than diplomatic:

1. **Would you, as a reviewer at a Q1 aerospace journal, recommend
   acceptance, major revision, or rejection?** And on what grounds?
2. **What is the single thing most likely to get it rejected?** Our own
   guess is the undemonstrated transition controllability, but you may see
   something else.
3. **Is there anything left that would materially raise its chances and
   that does not require a wind tunnel or an HPC cluster?** If the honest
   answer is "no — the remaining work is experimental," that is the answer
   we need. We would rather hear it now.
4. **Conversely: is there something we are undervaluing?** The three-bill
   framing, the escape condition, and the three-contract comparison are
   arguably novel contributions independent of whether this particular
   aircraft flies. If the paper's real contribution is the framework
   rather than the vehicle, the paper may need restructuring to say so —
   and that would be a significant piece of advice.

You have been right on every substantive call in this collaboration,
including two occasions where you caught arithmetic we had got wrong.
Please be as exacting here as you were there.
