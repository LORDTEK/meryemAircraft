## 2. The five lists

### 2.1 Grok — 12

1. **The gap the mission sits in.** Rotorcraft take off without a strip and cruise poorly; runway-dependent fixed-wing aircraft cruise on a wing and need a strip. The missions want both.
2. **Why hybrids have not cancelled the gap.** The penalty is architectural, in three coupled bills. Remedies move the tax.
3. **Escape condition.** Same hardware, same orientation, same job, hover peak from a buffer. *"Without this, 'airframe rotates' has no test."*
4. **First half: runway-independent vertical operation.** Opponent: runway-dependent fixed-wing. **Sized, not shown to be flyable.**
5. **Second half: wing-borne cruise.** Opponent: multirotors. *"No range race with fixed-wing aircraft."*
6. **The combination — own milestone.** Both halves at once *because* one propulsion set keeps one orientation and the airframe changes attitude. *"If this is only implied inside a configuration catalogue, the paper becomes either a vehicle description or a tax essay."*
7. **What the combination is made of, and what still moves.** Nose pair, tip pairs, no pivot or hub or elevon or rudder; roll from one strip, including its pitching moment. A count of eliminated classes — not "mechanically simpler."
8. **Soundness against numbers this project did not generate.** NASA sizing set; Bacchini.
9. **Price on this airframe.** The three bills as they actually fall here.
10. **What the numbers may be used to claim.** Three contracts. *"Without this the fourth axis leaks back in."*
11. **What is not closed.**
12. **Stop on the four axes.** No new calculation.

**Grok's objection to the briefing:** *"'Claimed' on vertical operation is only safe
if milestone 4 keeps 'sized, not demonstrated.' **I would not write 'claimed, by
construction' in the paper.**"*

## 4. The six places you actually differ
